# Post-event routed export to BU owners

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 05, 06

## Question

After the convention, how do leads get to the right BU?

- **The pack.** What each BU owner receives — a filtered sheet, a CSV, an email with a table? What columns, sorted how?
- **What is included.** All leads for that BU, or only those above a score threshold? Do EXPLORATORY leads go to anyone?
- **Relationship to the master workbook.** Is the per-BU pack a view of the same cloud Excel file, or a separate generated artefact?
- **Who sends it, and when.** Automated on a schedule, or a human presses a button after the event? With no real owner mailboxes yet, automated sending may not be deliverable.
- **Consent gate.** Only leads who consented may be contacted. Where is that filter enforced — in the export, or upstream at capture?
- **The 24-hour promise.** The brief targets same-day contact for HOT leads, which a post-event export cannot meet. Does the live staff alert fully cover that, or does HOT need its own immediate route?

## Answer

### The pack — one file per BU, never one file for everyone

Each BU owner receives **their own `.xlsx`**, generated by the mechanism ticket 02 settled: built server-side with SheetJS/ExcelJS and written to Supabase Storage, full rewrite each time so there is no append contention.

**Settled by reasoning, not grilled:** a single master workbook forwarded to every owner would disclose every other BU's prospects to each of them. That is an unnecessary disclosure of personal data with no upside — Markets & Treasury has no reason to hold Transaction Banking's contacts. Per-BU files are the minimising choice, and they cost nothing extra because the generator is already filtering by BU.

A **master workbook** containing every lead is also produced, for the organiser only. It is the only artefact that contains `other_unrouted` leads.

File naming: `uob-booth-<bu_id>-<YYYY-MM-DD>.xlsx`.

### Columns and order

Per-BU packs, in reading order, most decision-useful first:

`Band` · `Score` · `Name` · `Company` · `Role` · `Email` · `Phone` · `Interest` · `Need` · `Timeline` · `Intent` · `CTA chosen` · `Submitted at` · `Handled`

`Primary BU` is dropped from per-BU packs — every row has the same value — and retained in the master.

**Sorted by `Score` descending, then `Submitted at` descending.** Band is the first column so triage is a glance rather than a calculation.

Option ids are rendered as human labels at export time (ticket 06 stores ids, never labels).

### What is included

**Every lead routed to that BU, regardless of band.** These people consented and named a real need; filtering out EXPLORATORY would mean someone who explicitly asked to be contacted is never contacted by anyone. With Band in the first column and rows sorted by score, the BU sets its own bar rather than having ours imposed.

`other_unrouted` leads — the two declared non-needs from ticket 05 — appear only in the organiser's master. They have no owner and nobody is asked to chase them.

Deduplication per ticket 06: group by lowercased email, keep the highest `final_score`, ties broken by latest `submitted_at`.

### The consent gate is upstream, with one exception enforced here

**Purpose consent needs no export filter.** Ticket 07 gates the write on the consent tick, so a lead that exists in the database has, by construction, consented to being contacted about this conversation. There is no unconsented row to exclude.

**Phone consent is different and must be enforced at the export boundary.** Phone carries its own separate channel consent (ticket 07) because PDPA Part 9 governs marketing calls and SMS. Therefore:

> **The `Phone` cell is empty wherever `consent_phone_given` is false — even when a phone number is stored.**

A BU owner cannot call a number they were never given. This makes the DNC position structural rather than dependent on someone reading a policy note, which is the only version of this that survives contact with a busy salesperson.

### Delivery — a button, and a human

A **Generate Packs** button on the organiser dashboard, behind the shared event PIN from ticket 01. It regenerates the master and every per-BU file, then lists them with download links. The **data owner** (ticket 19) forwards each pack to its BU owner.

Chosen because it is deliverable today. Automated emailing needs the BU owner mailbox list, which the map has ruled out of scope — building it now would ship dead configuration and a job that silently sends nothing, or worse, sends personal data to a wrong slot. The manual step also puts a human's eyes on the data before it leaves the system, which for a file of prospect PII is a feature rather than friction.

**Accepted cost:** someone must remember. This goes in ticket 15's runbook as a named duty, not an assumption.

### Schedule — and §13's promise is actually met

Packs are generated at the **close of every event day**, and once more after the event.

Because a day's packs are generated at that day's close, **every HOT lead reaches its BU within 24 hours of being captured** — including day three, whose leads land in the final pack that same evening. §13's same-day/24-hour target is satisfied for the whole event, not just the early days. This was not obvious before the schedule was fixed, and it is the reason a daily cadence is worth the forwarding effort.

Packs are **cumulative**, not incremental — each contains every lead to date. An owner always holds the complete picture, and `Submitted at` lets them find what is new. This also means a lost or unforwarded pack is self-healing: the next one supersedes it.

The live HOT alert on the Staff View remains the *primary* same-day mechanism. Connecting a visitor to a specialist in person, at the booth, beats any email — the daily pack is the backstop for HOT leads no specialist could see.

### A retention hole this creates, and how it is closed

Ticket 07 deletes `leads` rows 90 days after the event. **Generated packs are copies of that same personal data and are not in the `leads` table.** Without action, the database is purged on schedule while a dozen spreadsheets full of prospect PII sit in Supabase Storage indefinitely — the retention promise kept in letter and broken in substance.

Two consequences:

1. **The 90-day deletion job must also purge the generated packs from Supabase Storage.** Added to ticket 15's deletion duty.
2. **Forwarded copies are outside our control.** Each pack therefore carries a short **cover sheet** as its first tab, stating: the consent purpose these leads agreed to (follow-up on this conversation, not general marketing), the 90-day retention expectation, that an empty Phone cell means the visitor did not consent to calls or SMS, and that the recipient is responsible for their own copy from the moment it is forwarded.

That cover sheet is the only thing that travels with the data once it leaves, so it is where the constraints have to live.
